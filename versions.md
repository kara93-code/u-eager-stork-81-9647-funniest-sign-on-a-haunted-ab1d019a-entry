# Funniest Sign on a Haunted Server Room Door — 3 versions

Contest: u-eager-stork-81-9647-funniest-sign-on-a-haunted-ab1d019a
Gate: one line, 1–120 characters, must read as a real door sign.
Judge order: (1) clearly a sign on a haunted server room door, (2) real laugh
from IT life colliding with the supernatural, (3) sounds like a sign — short,
punchy, readable at a glance, (4) haunting specific and fresh, (5) clean aloud,
no filler.

## A — ghost in rack 7 (94 chars)

WARNING: The ghost in rack 7 unplugs a server at 3 a.m. Do not apologise to it. It likes that.

Why: most server-room specific of the three (a numbered rack, a 3 a.m.
unplug, a behaviour rule for staff). The twist — apologising encourages it —
lands on the last three words. "WARNING:" is the most official sign voice.

## B — cold aisle ghost (98 chars)

NOTICE: The ghost haunts the cold aisle only. Keep this door shut. A warm ghost is a grumpy ghost.

Why: the sign's rule (keep the door shut) is a real server-room rule, and the
ghost's territory is exactly the cold aisle. The closer is a dry little
slogan — warm ghost, grumpy ghost — quotable, but the laugh is a smile more
than a laugh.

## C — do not knock (111 chars) — recommended

DO NOT KNOCK. The servers have started whispering again, and the ghost answers the door faster than IT support.

Why: fastest laugh of the three. "DO NOT KNOCK" is pure door-sign voice, the
whispering servers are a fresh server-room haunting, and the punch — the ghost
out-responds IT support — is the sharpest collision of IT life and the
supernatural here. Every tired sysadmin has waited on IT support; the ghost
being quicker is instantly, painfully relatable. Twist lands on the last two
words.
